# Academic Regulations for the B.Tech. Programme (2026-27)

Shivalik Institute of Technology, Indore
Document code: AR | Approved by the Academic Council at its 12th meeting, 18 June 2026

## 1. Preamble and Applicability

### 1.1 Scope
These Regulations govern the four-year Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech.) programme offered by the Institute in all branches of engineering. They apply to every student admitted to the programme from the academic year 2026-27 onwards and, unless otherwise notified, to students admitted earlier who have not yet completed the programme.

### 1.2 Definitions
In these Regulations, unless the context requires otherwise: "Institute" means Shivalik Institute of Technology, Indore; "Academic Council" means the Academic Council constituted under the Institute Statutes; "Dean" means the Dean (Academics) unless a different Dean is named; "HoD" means the Head of the Department offering the course; "course" means a subject of study identified by a course code and carrying a specified number of credits; "semester" means a period of about eighteen weeks of instruction and examination; and "working day" means a day on which the Institute office is open, excluding Sundays and declared holidays.

### 1.3 Related Documents
These Regulations are to be read together with the Examination and Evaluation Regulations, the Hostel Handbook, the Fee Schedule and Refund Rules, the Scholarship and Fee Concession Policy, and the Leave and Medical Policy for Students, each of which is issued separately by the office concerned.

## 2. Programme Structure

### 2.1 Duration
The B.Tech. programme is of four academic years, organised into eight semesters. A student must complete all requirements of the programme within a maximum period of seven academic years from the date of first admission, failing which the student's name shall be removed from the rolls of the Institute.

### 2.2 Credit Requirement
A student must earn a minimum of 160 credits to be eligible for the award of the B.Tech. degree. Credits are assigned to courses as follows: one credit for each hour per week of lecture (L) or tutorial (T), and one credit for every two hours per week of practical (P) work. A course with an L-T-P structure of 3-1-0 therefore carries four credits.

### 2.3 Academic Calendar
Each academic year has two semesters. The odd semester ordinarily commences in the third week of July and ends in December; the even semester ordinarily commences in the first week of January and ends in May. The academic calendar for each year is published by the Dean at least four weeks before the commencement of the odd semester and specifies the dates of registration, the instructional period, the mid-term tests, the end-semester examinations and the vacations.

### 2.4 Categories of Courses
Courses are classified as Basic Science, Engineering Science, Humanities and Social Science, Programme Core, Programme Elective, Open Elective, and Project. Programme Electives and Open Electives are offered subject to a minimum enrolment of ten students, and a student is allotted electives in the order of the student's preference and the availability of seats.

## 3. Registration

### 3.1 Semester Registration
Every student must register for courses at the beginning of each semester on the dates notified in the academic calendar. Registration is complete only when the prescribed fees have been paid and the registration form has been approved by the Faculty Advisor. A student who does not register in a semester shall not be permitted to attend classes or to appear for any examination of that semester.

### 3.2 Late Registration
A student who fails to register on the notified dates may register late, on payment of a late registration fee of Rs. 500, within ten working days of the commencement of the semester. No registration shall be permitted after this period, and such a student shall be treated as having taken a break of study for that semester.

### 3.3 Credit Load
A student shall register for a minimum of 16 credits and a maximum of 28 credits in a semester. The Dean may permit a student with a CGPA of 8.5 or above to register for up to 32 credits in a semester in order to complete the programme early or to pursue a minor.

### 3.4 Add and Drop
A student may add or drop a course within the first two weeks of the semester with the approval of the Faculty Advisor, provided that the credit load after the change remains within the limits specified in Regulation 3.3. A course dropped within this period does not appear on the grade card.

## 4. Attendance

### 4.1 Minimum Attendance
A student must have a minimum of 75 per cent attendance in each course, computed separately for the theory and practical components, in order to be eligible to appear for the end-semester examination in that course. Attendance is computed from the first day of instruction of the semester to the last instructional day, and includes all lectures, tutorials and practical sessions scheduled for the course.

### 4.2 Monitoring and Warning
Course instructors shall upload attendance on the academic portal every week. The HoD shall issue a written warning to any student whose attendance falls below 80 per cent at the end of the sixth week and again at the end of the tenth week of instruction. A copy of each warning shall be sent to the student's parent or guardian.

### 4.3 Condonation of Shortage of Attendance
A shortage of attendance may be condoned by the Dean on medical grounds only, provided that the student's attendance in the course concerned is not less than 65 per cent, that the illness is supported by a medical certificate issued by a registered medical practitioner and countersigned by the Institute Medical Officer, and that the certificate is submitted to the HoD within seven days of the student rejoining classes. A shortage of attendance below 65 per cent shall not be condoned under any circumstances.

### 4.4 Detention
A student who does not satisfy the attendance requirement of Regulation 4.1 and whose shortage is not condoned under Regulation 4.3 shall be detained in that course. A detained student shall not be permitted to appear for the end-semester examination in that course, shall be awarded the grade "DT", and must re-register for the course when it is next offered.

### 4.5 Attendance during Institute Deputation
A student deputed by the Dean (Student Welfare) to represent the Institute in an inter-university or inter-collegiate competition, a conference, or an NCC or NSS camp shall be marked present for the duration of the deputation, provided that the deputation letter is issued before the event and is submitted to the HoD within three working days of the student's return.

## 5. Scheme of Evaluation

### 5.1 Theory Courses
Every theory course is evaluated for 100 marks, of which 40 marks are allotted to continuous internal assessment and 60 marks to the end-semester examination conducted by the Controller of Examinations.

### 5.2 Components of Internal Assessment
The internal assessment of 40 marks in a theory course comprises two mid-term tests of 15 marks each, conducted in the sixth and twelfth weeks of instruction, and 10 marks for assignments, quizzes or tutorials as announced by the instructor in the first week of the semester. Internal assessment marks shall be displayed to students at least one week before the end-semester examination, and any discrepancy must be reported to the instructor within three working days of the display.

### 5.3 Practical Courses
Every practical course is evaluated for 100 marks, of which 60 marks are allotted to continuous internal assessment based on the laboratory record, day-to-day performance and an internal test, and 40 marks to the end-semester practical examination conducted jointly by an internal and an external examiner.

### 5.4 Passing Requirement
To pass a course a student must secure a minimum of 30 per cent of the marks in the end-semester examination and a minimum of 40 per cent of the total marks, that is, the internal assessment and the end-semester examination taken together. A student who does not satisfy either requirement shall be awarded the grade "F" in that course.

### 5.5 Project Work
The final-year project is evaluated for 200 marks: 100 marks by the internal guide through periodic reviews and 100 marks by a committee at the end-semester viva voce examination. The project report must be submitted at least ten days before the viva voce, in the format prescribed by the Department.

## 6. Grading

### 6.1 Letter Grades
Marks obtained in a course are converted to letter grades with grade points as follows:

| Letter grade | Grade points | Range of marks (out of 100) |
|---|---|---|
| O (Outstanding) | 10 | 90 and above |
| A+ (Excellent) | 9 | 80 to 89 |
| A (Very Good) | 8 | 70 to 79 |
| B+ (Good) | 7 | 60 to 69 |
| B (Above Average) | 6 | 50 to 59 |
| C (Average) | 5 | 40 to 49 |
| F (Fail) | 0 | Below 40, or below 30 per cent in the end-semester examination |
| DT (Detained) | 0 | Detained for shortage of attendance |

### 6.2 SGPA and CGPA
The Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) is the credit-weighted average of the grade points of all courses registered in a semester. The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is the credit-weighted average of the grade points of all courses registered in all semesters completed so far. Both are computed to two decimal places and are not rounded up. Courses in which the grade is F or DT are included in the computation with zero grade points until the course is passed.

### 6.3 Grade Card
A grade card showing the courses registered, the credits, the grades, the SGPA and the CGPA shall be issued to every student within thirty days of the declaration of the results of each semester. The consolidated grade card and the provisional degree certificate are issued after the student has completed all programme requirements and obtained a no-dues certificate.

## 7. Promotion and Academic Probation

### 7.1 Promotion to the Third Year
A student shall be promoted to the third year (fifth semester) only if the student has earned at least 50 per cent of the total credits prescribed for the first and second years taken together.

### 7.2 Promotion to the Fourth Year
A student shall be promoted to the fourth year (seventh semester) only if the student has earned all the credits prescribed for the first year and at least 50 per cent of the total credits prescribed for the second and third years taken together.

### 7.3 Academic Probation
A student who is not promoted under Regulation 7.1 or 7.2 shall be placed on academic probation and shall register only for the backlog courses in the following semester. A student on academic probation shall be assigned a faculty mentor and shall not hold any office in a student body during the period of probation.

## 8. Supplementary Examinations

### 8.1 Eligibility
A student who has obtained the grade F in a course may appear for a supplementary examination in that course. Supplementary examinations are held within forty-five days of the declaration of the results of the semester concerned, on dates notified by the Controller of Examinations.

### 8.2 Carry-forward of Internal Marks
The internal assessment marks obtained in the regular semester shall be carried forward to the supplementary examination. No separate internal assessment is conducted for supplementary examinations.

### 8.3 Grades in Supplementary Examinations
Grades obtained in a supplementary examination are entered on the grade card with the suffix "S". There is no ceiling on the grade that may be obtained in a supplementary examination.

### 8.4 Number of Attempts
A student may appear in the supplementary examination of a course any number of times within the maximum duration of the programme specified in Regulation 2.1, on payment of the prescribed fee for each attempt.

## 9. Re-evaluation and Re-totalling

### 9.1 Application
A student who is not satisfied with the marks awarded in the end-semester examination of a theory course may apply to the Controller of Examinations for re-evaluation of the answer script within ten working days of the declaration of results, on payment of the prescribed fee per course. No application shall be entertained after this period. Re-evaluation is not available for practical examinations, project evaluations or internal assessment.

### 9.2 Procedure and Refund of Fee
The answer script shall be evaluated afresh by an examiner other than the original examiner. If the marks change by ten per cent or more of the maximum marks of the end-semester examination, the re-evaluation fee shall be refunded to the student.

### 9.3 Finality
The marks awarded on re-evaluation, whether higher or lower than the original marks, shall be final and shall replace the original marks. No further review of a re-evaluated answer script is permitted.

## 10. Break of Study

### 10.1 Grounds
A student may be permitted a break of study of one semester or one academic year on medical grounds, certified by the Institute Medical Officer and approved by the Dean. A break of study shall not be granted on any other ground and shall not be granted more than once during the programme.

### 10.2 Application
An application for a break of study must be submitted to the Dean before the commencement of the semester for which the break is sought or, in the case of an illness arising during a semester, within fifteen days of the onset of the illness. A student on a break of study is not required to pay tuition fees for the semester of the break but must pay the continuation fee prescribed in the Fee Schedule.

### 10.3 Effect on Duration
The period of a break of study counts towards the maximum duration of the programme specified in Regulation 2.1. On rejoining, the student shall be governed by the regulations and the curriculum in force for the batch with which the student rejoins.

## 11. Scholarships and Medals

### 11.1 Merit Scholarship
The Institute awards a Merit Scholarship to students ranking in the top five per cent of each branch on the basis of CGPA at the end of each academic year. The scholarship takes the form of a waiver of fifty per cent of the tuition fee for the following academic year.

### 11.2 Continuation of Merit Scholarship
A Merit Scholarship once awarded shall continue for the following academic year provided that the student maintains a CGPA of not less than 7.5 at the end of each semester and is not subjected to any disciplinary action. A student whose CGPA falls below 7.5 in any semester forfeits the scholarship for the remaining period of that academic year.

### 11.3 Medals
The Chancellor's Gold Medal is awarded to the student securing the highest CGPA across all branches in the graduating batch, and a Branch Silver Medal to the topper of each branch, provided that the student has completed the programme in four years without any F or DT grade and without any disciplinary action.

## 12. Fees and Dues

### 12.1 Payment of Fees
Semester fees shall be paid on or before the dates specified in the Fee Schedule published by the Finance Office. Registration for a semester is provisional until the fees for that semester have been paid in full.

### 12.2 Consequence of Outstanding Tuition Fees
A student who has outstanding tuition fee dues on the last date of payment shall not be issued a hall ticket for the end-semester examination and shall not be permitted to appear for it. Hall tickets withheld under this Regulation shall be released within one working day of the dues being cleared, provided that the examination has not yet commenced.

### 12.3 No-dues Certificate
The degree certificate, the consolidated grade card and the transfer certificate shall be issued only after the student has obtained a no-dues certificate from the Library, the Hostel Office, the Department and the Finance Office.

## 13. Academic Misconduct

### 13.1 Unfair Means in Examinations
The use of unfair means in any examination, test or evaluation shall be dealt with in accordance with the Examination and Evaluation Regulations of the Institute.

### 13.2 Impersonation and Forgery
A student found to have impersonated another student in any examination, or to have arranged for another person to impersonate the student, or to have forged or altered any Institute document, shall be expelled from the Institute and the matter shall be reported to the police.

## 14. Power to Relax

### 14.1 Relaxation by the Academic Council
Notwithstanding anything contained in these Regulations, the Academic Council may, in exceptional cases and for reasons to be recorded in writing, relax any provision of these Regulations in respect of an individual student. An application for relaxation shall be made through the HoD and the Dean, and the decision of the Academic Council shall be final.

## 15. Interpretation and Amendment

### 15.1 Interpretation
If any question arises as to the interpretation of these Regulations, or as to the effect of any inconsistency between these Regulations and any other policy of the Institute, the decision of the Vice-Chancellor shall be final.

### 15.2 Amendment
The Academic Council may amend these Regulations from time to time. Amendments take effect from the academic year following their approval unless the Academic Council directs otherwise.
